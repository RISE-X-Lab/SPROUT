SPROUT: Scaling Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon Software Engineering

SPROUT checkpoints the environment and the agent context after every turn. When a rollout fails, the same policy reads the failure, picks the turn before the mistake, and continues from there.

    01Why

    One wrong turn zeroes the rollout. Restarting throws away the rest.

    A long-horizon attempt reads an unfamiliar codebase, installs dependencies, builds and runs tests, sometimes for hours. The verifier's reward is binary, so one bad decision anywhere makes it zero, and in group-relative RL a task whose attempts all fail contributes no gradient. The hardest tasks burn the most compute and teach the least.

    Resample from scratch

    Every new attempt redoes the exploration and the setup, and may repeat the same mistake. Branching only inside the conversation does not help either: the files the agent edited, the packages it installed and the artifacts it built are gone.

    Pass@4 = 4× the tokens for a few more solved tasks

    Branch from a checkpoint

    Keep the turns before the mistake. Restore the environment snapshot and the agent context to exactly that boundary, let the verifier's feedback decide where and how to continue, and spend new turns only on what was wrong.

    SPROUT beats Pass@4 with 32–52% of its tokens
    02Method

    Two halves of a checkpoint, one policy in three roles, one group to train on.

    A checkpoint is a turn boundary with two halves: the environment snapshot after the turn's tool calls, and the agent context up to the same observation. Restoring both gives a continuation the exact state of its prefix, with nothing re-executed.

    i

    Restorable execution layer

    Each attempt runs in its own microVM. After every turn the environment's changed disk blocks are saved as an incremental snapshot layer, so a checkpoint grows with what the turn wrote, not with the size of the environment. Tool calls are synchronous shell commands with no background processes, so nothing persists only in memory and guest memory is never stored. Branches write to their own copy-on-write layer.

    ii

    Verifier-guided backtracking

    The actor also plays analyst and reviewer under role prompts; the roles share weights. The analyst reads a failed attempt with the verifier's diagnostics. The reviewer picks a parent attempt and a boundary and writes the starting turn that replaces the parent's turn there. Two rounds, 4 then 3 branches, stopping at the first pass.

    iii

    GRPO on branched rollouts

    Each task gets two roots and three children per root. When both roots fail, the children start with reviewer-written turns; otherwise they continue unguided from a chosen boundary. Successes are credited, shared prefixes are counted once, and the analysis never enters the learner's input, so the policy learns the corrective turn from its ordinary context.

    One policy, three prompts: who sees what

    RoleReadsWrites
    Actorthe task and its own history in the sandboxa turn: reasoning, then shell calls

    Reviewerall analyses, the attempts' histories, earlier starting turns and their outcomesfor each branch: a parent, a boundary, and the starting turn in the actor's voice

    The three roles share weights; only the analyst and reviewer prompts carry verifier diagnostics. Boundaries lie between turns, never inside one: within a turn the actor has already committed to all of its calls.

    04Depth vs breadth

    More rounds beat more branches, and cost less.

    With the same budget of branches, more review rounds with fewer branches each resolve more tasks for fewer tokens, because each round starts from the previous round's failures and their diagnostics. Breadth alone saturates: 32 branches in one round barely improve on 4, though they still beat eight independent attempts with a third fewer tokens.

    Eight branches in one round: 76.1% for 0.75B tokens. The same eight over eight rounds: 92.0% for 0.53B, recovering 52 instead of 34 of the 61 tasks the baseline fails. Widening one round from 4 to 32 branches adds 3.5 points for 3.2× the tokens. Depth does add sequential dependencies between rounds; these runs measure the success–token trade-off, not latency.

    Why branching works

    i

    Partial progress is worth keeping

    A trajectory passing 45 of 46 tests scores the same as one passing none, yet it has localized the problem to one test. Of 26 branch points in the SWE-bench Pro case study, 21 fall in the middle or final third of the parent's progress.

    ii

    The starting turn decides

    Four Ansible branches resume from the same checkpoint and only one passes 46 of 46: the one whose fix matches the caller's real constraints, which the verifier's error message exposed and the agent's own assumptions did not.

    iii

    Failed branches seed the next round

    On the Flipt ECR task, 4 baseline attempts, 7 BPO branches and 7 Shepherd branches all fail. SPROUT's first round fails 2 of 6 tests; the verifier names two undefined constants; the second round resumes from that branch and passes in ten more turns.
